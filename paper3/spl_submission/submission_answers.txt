[Why within SPL scope] (max 150 words)
This Letter addresses a signal-processing problem: single-channel
blind source separation of co-frequency communication signals, with
per-source modulation detection at the receiver. Its contribution
concerns how detection performance in such systems is measured: we
show that SNR-conditioned evaluation—a standard reporting practice
across signal processing—fails silently under label–score
misalignment, formalize the failure with a closed-form analysis, and
validate it on a full co-frequency separation pipeline with
communication-signal models (PSK/QAM, RRC shaping, multipath, AWGN).
The resulting audit protocol applies to any signal-processing study
that reports per-condition metrics. The machine-learning components
(OOD scorers, embeddings) are the instruments evaluated, applied to a
core signal-processing task; the contribution itself is an evaluation
theory and protocol for signal-processing systems.

[Why significant / impact] (max 150 words)
Per-condition reporting—AUROC, error rates, or gains broken down by
SNR, SIR, or another operating variable—is ubiquitous across signal
processing, from communications receivers to radar and biomedical
sensing. We show that this practice fails silently under a one-line
indexing mismatch: deterministic label–score misalignment manufactures
convincing per-condition trends that survive multi-seed averaging,
significance tests, and robustness batteries, the exact safeguards the
community trusts. The significance is twofold. Scientifically, we
prove the confound is general (a closed-form Gaussian corollary and a
domain-free toy require no separator, signal, or detector), so any
condition-conditioned metric is at risk, and our own retracted result
illustrates the damage. Practically, the Letter supplies a zero-cost
audit protocol—label regeneration checks, strict train/reference/test
separation, permutation-null calibration—that authors, reviewers, and
readers can apply immediately to certify existing and future
evaluations.

[Distinctive/new relative to the three works] (max 150 words)
Hou and Gao design separation algorithms; we do not propose a
separator—we show how per-source detection performance in such
systems must be evaluated, and how the standard practice fails. Li
et al. build open-set modulation recognizers; we show that the
SNR-conditioned evaluation of exactly such detectors can silently
manufacture or erase detection margins through deterministic
label–score misalignment. Kapoor and Narayanan catalog data leakage
in machine-learning-based science broadly; we isolate a distinct
mechanism they do not cover—an indexing permutation interacting with
condition-conditioned metrics—prove it formally with a closed-form
corollary and a domain-free toy, demonstrate it inside a full
communication-signal pipeline, and show it evades every safeguard
they recommend (multi-seed averaging, significance tests). The audit
protocol we distill is the first targeted at this failure class.
