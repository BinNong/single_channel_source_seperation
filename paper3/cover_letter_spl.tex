\textbf{Manuscript title:} A Label--Score Alignment Pitfall in SNR-Conditioned OOD Detection for Single-Channel Blind Source Separation\\
\textbf{Manuscript type:} Letter (original submission)

\vspace{0.6em}

\noindent Dear Editor,

We submit this Letter reporting an evaluation-methodology pitfall in
SNR-conditioned out-of-distribution (OOD) detection for single-channel
blind source separation (SC-BSS) of co-frequency communication signals.
In our own research pipeline, two deterministic label--score
misalignments---neither detectable by multi-seed averaging, significance
tests, or robustness batteries---simultaneously manufactured a spurious
SNR-routing gain (AUROC $0.50 \rightarrow 0.625$) and erased a genuine
same-mixture detection margin ($0.54 \rightarrow$ chance). We formalize
the failure as an indexing-permutation confound (Proposition~1 with a
closed-form Gaussian corollary) and show with a domain-free analytical
toy that it needs no separator, signal, or detector: it is a general
confound of condition-conditioned evaluation, directly relevant to the
many signal-processing studies that report per-SNR or per-condition
metrics. The Letter closes with a minimal audit protocol that catches
both failure modes at zero cost.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is
not under consideration by any other journal. No part of it has been
posted as a preprint. All authors have approved the submission. Code,
score dumps, and a one-click label-audit script reproducing every
reported number are publicly available (link in the manuscript).

We hope you find the Letter suitable for \emph{IEEE Signal Processing
Letters}.
