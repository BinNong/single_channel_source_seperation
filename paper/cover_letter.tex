\noindent Dear Editor,

\vspace{0.4em}

We are pleased to submit the above manuscript for consideration as an original research article in \emph{Wireless Personal Communications}. The paper proposes a lightweight complex-valued CNN with a Complex Squeeze-and-Excitation (C-SE) attention block for single-channel blind source separation of co-frequency communication signals, and evaluates it against both convolutional and state-space baselines. We believe the topic fits the journal's scope in wireless communications, signal processing, and interference mitigation.

\paragraph{Key strengths of the manuscript.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Novel complex-native attention.} The C-SE block jointly pools real and imaginary statistics and produces a \emph{real-valued} channel weight that scales complex features while preserving their phase---to the best of our knowledge, a phase-preserving, real-gated design not previously evaluated for lightweight single-channel blind separation of near-co-frequency communication signals.
\item \textbf{Lightweight and deployable.} The full model has only 235K parameters and trains in minutes on a single \SI{8}{GB} GPU (NVIDIA RTX 4060), making it suitable for resource-constrained edge devices and software-defined radios.
\item \textbf{Rigorous multi-seed evaluation.} Five random seeds with sample standard deviations are reported for the proposed model and the parameter-matched baselines, together with paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on per-seed SDR. We are explicit that no comparison reaches statistical significance at $\alpha=0.05$ and position the model honestly on per-parameter efficiency and partial collapse-resistance rather than claiming raw SDR dominance.
\item \textbf{Comprehensive baselines and ablations.} Four modulation types (BPSK, QPSK, 8PSK, 16QAM), a complex-domain Conv-TasNet, parameter-matched real-valued and no-SE ablations, and scaled-down re-implementations of two cross-domain communication-signal baselines (CNSE, S4-UNET); ablations isolate the SE block, the scale mode, and the squeeze pooling.
\item \textbf{Generalisation to the co-frequency limit.} A model retrained on carrier-gap distributions $U(0, \SI{5}{Hz})$ holds $\text{SDR}\approx\SI{3.3}{dB}$ across $[0, \SI{500}{Hz}]$, including the previously unseen co-frequency limit $\Delta f=\SI{0}{Hz}$.
\item \textbf{Reproducibility.} The training, evaluation, and figure-generation code is publicly available at \url{https://github.com/BinNong/single_channel_source_seperation} under the MIT license; per-seed numbers, exact commands, and hyperparameters are recorded in \texttt{docs/EXPERIMENT\_LOG.md}, and pre-trained checkpoints will be made publicly available upon publication.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Headline numbers ($\text{SNR}=\SI{10}{dB}$).}
The proposed C-SE reaches $\text{SDR}=\SI{2.31 \pm 0.62}{dB}$ at 235K parameters (five seeds). The matched no-SE complex CNN reaches $\SI{1.81 \pm 0.48}{dB}$ (five seeds); the matched real-valued CNN reaches $\SI{2.20 \pm 0.59}{dB}$ (five seeds); the complex Conv-TasNet reaches $\SI{3.23 \pm 0.40}{dB}$ (three seeds, 817K parameters). CNSE (6.69M parameters) reaches $\SI{2.38 \pm 0.91}{dB}$ and S4-UNET (1.57M) reaches $\SI{3.05 \pm 0.00}{dB}$ (three seeds each). C-SE is therefore competitive at the 235K-parameter scale on per-parameter efficiency, partial collapse-resistance, and predictable runtime.
