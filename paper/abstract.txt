Single-channel blind source separation (SC-BSS) of co-frequency communication signals is an ill-posed problem with applications in spectrum surveillance, cognitive radio, and interference mitigation. We propose a lightweight complex-valued convolutional neural network (CNN) with a Complex Squeeze-and-Excitation (C-SE) block that operates directly on the in-phase/quadrature (I/Q) representation of communication signals. C-SE jointly pools real and imaginary statistics through a shared excitation network and produces a real-valued channel weight that scales complex features while preserving phase. At only 235K parameters, the model achieves a signal-to-distortion ratio (SDR) of 2.31 ± 0.62 dB over five random seeds across four modulation types — binary phase-shift keying (BPSK), quadrature phase-shift keying (QPSK), 8-phase-shift keying (8PSK), and 16-ary quadrature amplitude modulation (16QAM) on 5 Hz co-frequency mixtures — roughly an order of magnitude smaller than our scaled-down re-implementations of the CNSE and S4-UNET baselines (6.69M and 1.57M parameters, SDR 2.38 dB and 3.05 dB) and within ~1 dB of the 817K-parameter Complex Conv-TasNet (3.23 dB). Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on common seeds give paired ΔSDR between −0.48 dB and +0.50 dB, none reaching α = 0.05, so we make no inferential claim of superiority or equivalence and instead position C-SE as a competitive operating point at the 235K-parameter scale in partial collapse-resistance, per-parameter efficiency, and predictable runtime. A C-SE model retrained on Δf ~ U(0, 5 Hz) generalises to a flat SDR ≈ 3.3 dB across the entire [0, 500 Hz] range, including the unseen co-frequency limit Δf = 0 Hz. These results show that phase-preserving complex attention at the 235K-parameter scale is a viable design point for communication-signal BSS, with per-seed variance reported for honest comparison.
