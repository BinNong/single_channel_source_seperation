Single-channel blind source separation (SC-BSS) of co-frequency communication signals is trained and ranked by waveform-fidelity metrics such as scale-invariant signal-to-distortion ratio (SI-SDR), yet the downstream task is demodulation. This paper contributes a bit-level evaluation protocol for SC-BSS—an offset-compensated receiver producing compensated symbol error rate (SER) and Gray-mapped bit error rate from shared decisions—together with a differentiable soft-demodulation loss verified decision-identical to that receiver, and applies them in a controlled five-seed study on a variable-count (K in {1,2,3}) co-frequency benchmark. The protocol exposes a large waveform-to-bit mismatch: a +4.1 dB SI-SDR improvement moves the compensated SER from 0.5049 to 0.5027—even +8.4 dB at −10 dB SNR buys nothing—because the mixture is interference-limited at SIR approximately 0 dB. The task-oriented loss is negligible in the pool (at most 0.03 percentage points in the fair additive comparison) but sign-structured by the interference order: it helps at K=2 (5/5 seeds positive in all four contrasts) and hurts at K=3 (0/5 seeds). Deprived of the waveform anchor, the loss collapses into reward hacking (−9.4 dB), delimiting where the proxy can be trusted; with the anchor, the same term repairs occupancy gating (count accuracy 0.65 to 0.85). A joint soft-information head fails at chance, and controlled oracle-versus-blind probes attribute the failure to burst-level synchronisation and phase-entangled joint detection. Produced under a pre-declared statistical protocol and released with full code, these results provide reusable measurement infrastructure and a concrete prescription: closing the waveform-to-bit gap requires explicit synchronisation and equalisation structure, not a better loss weight.
