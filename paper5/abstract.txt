Single-channel blind source separation (SC-BSS) of co-frequency communication signals is trained and ranked by waveform-fidelity metrics such as the scale-invariant signal-to-distortion ratio (SI-SDR), yet the downstream task is demodulation. On a variable-count (K in {1,2,3}) benchmark scored by an offset-compensated receiver (oracle carrier synchronisation, matched filtering, bit error rate, BER), we quantify the mismatch: a +4.1 dB SI-SDR improvement moves the compensated symbol error rate (SER) from 0.5049 to 0.5027—even +8.4 dB at -10 dB SNR buys nothing (a slight penalty if anything)—because the mixture is interference-limited at SIR approximately 0 dB. We then test a task-oriented loss: a differentiable soft-demodulation term decision-identical to the evaluation receiver, within the variable-K permutation-invariant loss. The pooled effect across five seeds is negligible (0.03 percentage points (pp) at most, fair additive comparison) but hides sign structure along the interference axis: the term helps at K=2 (BER +0.06 to +0.40 pp, 5/5 seeds positive in all four) and hurts at K=3 (SER -0.6 to -0.7 pp, 0/5 seeds in all four); the number of interferers, not the loss, sets the sign. Removing the waveform objective collapses the model (reward hacking; SI-SDR improvement -9.4 dB): waveform supervision is necessary but not sufficient. The term also regularises source counting (occupancy-routed count accuracy 0.65 to 0.85; hallucination 0.15 to 0.05-0.06). A joint soft-information head bypassing explicit separation fails at chance; a controlled probe attributes this to burst-level blind carrier/phase recovery. Closing the waveform-to-bit gap requires explicit synchronisation and equalisation structure, not a better loss weight.
