\noindent Dear Editor,

\vspace{0.4em}

We are pleased to submit the above manuscript for consideration in
\emph{AEÜ}. The paper is a \emph{measurement study with an evaluation
protocol contribution}: it asks, for single-channel blind source
separation (SC-BSS) of co-frequency communication signals, whether the
waveform-fidelity gains that the field optimises and reports
(SI-SDR/SDR) actually reach the demodulated bits---and, if not, whether
a task-oriented loss can make them. This question sits squarely within
AEÜ's readership: it connects the deep-learning separation literature to
the classical co-frequency interference-cancellation and PCMA line of
work, and it concerns exactly the physical-layer receivers your
community builds. We ask you to consider it as a measurement/protocol
paper whose negative results are \emph{diagnosed}, not merely reported.

\paragraph{Key contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A reproducible empirical law along the interference axis.}
On a variable-count ($K \in \{1,2,3\}$) co-frequency benchmark scored by
an offset-compensated receiver (compensated SER and Gray-mapped BER), a
$+4.1$\,dB SI-SDR improvement moves SER only from 0.5049 to 0.5027---
even $+8.4$\,dB at $-10$\,dB SNR buys nothing (if anything a
fraction-of-a-point penalty)---because the mixture is
interference-limited at $\mathrm{SIR} \approx 0$\,dB (one interferer
raises the baseline SER $3.8\times$, from 0.146 to 0.561). A
differentiable soft-demodulation loss whose receiver front-end is
line-by-line identical to the evaluation receiver changes the pooled
bit error rate by at most 0.03\,pp in the fair additive comparison---
but the pool hides a sign structure: the term \emph{helps} at $K{=}2$
($+0.35$\,pp BER, 5/5 seeds positive in all four contrasts) and
\emph{hurts} at $K{=}3$ ($-0.6$ to $-0.7$\,pp SER, 0/5 seeds positive),
cancelling under the 1:2:3 pair weighting. The number of interferers,
not the loss, sets the sign of what little effect exists---an
experimental variable we are not aware of being isolated before in this
literature.
\item \textbf{An evaluation protocol and statistical standard.} The
training-time soft receiver and the evaluation receiver share one
implementation, removing the train/eval mismatch that has made earlier
SER claims hard to compare; all contrasts use an exact sign-flip
permutation test at the seed level and a Monte-Carlo permutation test at
the cell level, with Bonferroni control over eight pre-declared
contrasts and the $n{=}5$ power ceiling ($p \geq 0.0625$ attainable)
stated explicitly rather than smoothed over. We believe this protocol is
reusable wherever separation is evaluated against downstream bits.
\item \textbf{A controlled attribution experiment and a concrete
prescription.} The strongest task-oriented design---a joint
soft-information head that bypasses explicit separation---fails at the
modulation-averaged chance level, and a controlled probe (same head,
same data, oracle vs.\ blind synchronisation as the only variable)
attributes the failure to burst-level blind carrier/phase recovery
rather than to model capacity. The practical conclusion for the
co-frequency community is direct: closing the waveform-to-bit gap
requires explicit synchronisation and equalisation structure---the
machinery of the interference-cancellation/PCMA tradition---not a better
loss weight.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{On reporting negative results.}
Several of our headline findings are nulls or adverse effects, and we
report them as such, with mechanism. We submit them because the
alternative---continuing to rank SC-BSS systems by SI-SDR alone---has a
measurable cost: our per-$K$ decomposition shows that pooled metrics can
hide opposite-signed effects, and our audit of the standard recipe shows
a configuration can be reward-hacked into waveforms that no receiver
would accept. We would rather the field learn this from data than from
another round of architecture papers optimising the wrong endpoint.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
All training, evaluation, analysis, and figure/table-generation code,
including the synthetic data generator and the deterministic test grid
(test seed 99999), is publicly available at
\url{https://github.com/BinNong/single_channel_source_seperation/tree/main/paper5_task_oriented};
no external datasets are required. Every headline number is multi-seed
(five seeds), and the exact command behind every number is recorded in
the repository's experiment log.

\paragraph{Related submissions.}
Two related companion manuscripts are under review elsewhere and share no
results with the present paper: a closed-set separator design (the
complex CNN with squeeze-and-excitation used as a baseline family here)
at \emph{Wireless Personal Communications} (Springer), and the
open-world variable-$K$ study that this paper's benchmark and slot
separator build upon at \emph{China Communications}. The present
manuscript is fully self-contained; the cited companion manuscript
(currently listed as ``under review'') is available to the editor and
reviewers on request.
